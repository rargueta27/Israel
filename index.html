The Roman Displacement of the Jewish People

The displacement of the Jewish people from their ancestral homeland was a long and traumatic process that unfolded under the rule of the Roman Empire.

Before the Roman Conquest

For centuries before Roman rule, the Jewish people lived in the land historically known as Judea, with their spiritual and national center in Jerusalem. The heart of Jewish religious life was the Second Temple.

The First Jewish–Roman War (66–73 CE)

  • Heavy Roman taxation
  • Religious interference
  • Harsh rule by Roman governors

Destruction of the Second Temple

  • Jerusalem was devastated
  • The Second Temple was burned
  • Many Jews were killed or enslaved

Bottom Line

The Roman wars against Judea led to massive displacement and the beginning of the long diaspora.

The Shoah (The Holocaust)

The Holocaust was the systematic murder of six million Jews by Nazi Germany between 1933 and 1945.

Rise of Nazi Hatred

  • Jews removed from government jobs
  • Businesses boycotted
  • Nuremberg Laws stripped citizenship

The Death Camps

  • Auschwitz-Birkenau
  • Treblinka
  • Sobibor

Bottom Line

The Holocaust was one of the darkest crimes in human history.

The Eternal Return — Rebuilding After Catastrophe

After the Holocaust, the Jewish people rebuilt their national life with determination and faith.

Reestablishing National Life

  • Economic austerity
  • Housing shortages
  • Security pressures

Blooming the Land

  • Precision irrigation
  • Greenhouse farming
  • Water recycling

Economic Prosperity

Israel developed into a high-income, innovation-driven economy.
