Israel Forever: The Eternal Return

Honoring the legacy of Sheldon and Miriam Adelson through the rhythm of our history.

The Roman Empire Invasion

The Roman Displacement of the Jewish People

The displacement of the Jewish people from their ancestral homeland was a long and traumatic process that unfolded under the rule of the Roman Empire. To understand it fully, we must look at what happened in Judea during the first and second centuries CE.

Before the Roman Conquest

For centuries before Roman rule, the Jewish people lived in the land historically known as Judea, with their spiritual and national center in Jerusalem. The heart of Jewish religious life was the Second Temple, rebuilt after the Babylonian exile. The Jewish population maintained a strong identity tied to their land, religion, and traditions.

The First Jewish–Roman War (66–73 CE)

Tensions between Jewish inhabitants and Roman authorities grew due to heavy Roman taxation, religious interference, and harsh rule by Roman governors. In 66 CE, a major Jewish revolt broke out.

Destruction of the Second Temple

In 70 CE, Roman forces led by Titus crushed the rebellion and destroyed Jerusalem’s Temple.

Impact:

Jerusalem was devastated • The Second Temple was burned • Many Jews were killed, enslaved, or forced to flee

Continued Resistance and the Bar Kokhba Revolt (132–136 CE)

Decades later, another major uprising erupted under Jewish leader Simon Bar Kokhba. The Roman response was extremely severe.

Aftermath included:

Massive loss of Jewish life, destruction of many Jewish towns, large-scale expulsions from Judea, and the renaming of the province to erase Jewish identity.

The Long Diaspora

Following these wars, Jewish communities spread throughout the Middle East, North Africa, Europe, and later the Americas.

Bottom Line

The Roman wars against Judea were devastating and led to the destruction of Jewish political sovereignty, large-scale death and displacement, and the beginning of the long global diaspora.

The Holocaust

The Holocaust (Shoah in Hebrew) was the systematic, state-sponsored persecution and murder of six million Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators between 1933 and 1945.

Rise of Nazi Hatred (1933–1939)

Jews were removed from government jobs, businesses were boycotted, and propaganda portrayed Jews as enemies. The Nuremberg Laws stripped Jews of citizenship.

Kristallnacht — The Night of Broken Glass (1938)

Synagogues were burned, Jewish shops destroyed, thousands of Jews arrested, and many killed.

The Death Camps

Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka, and Sobibor.

Bottom Line

The Holocaust was one of the darkest crimes in human history. Despite immense suffering, Jewish communities demonstrated remarkable resilience and survival.

The Eternal Return

After the devastation of the Holocaust, the Jewish people rebuilt their national life with determination, faith, and unity.

Reestablishing National Life (1948)

Faced with severe economic austerity, food rationing, housing shortages, and security pressures with limited natural resources.

Blooming the Land

Innovation in precision irrigation, advanced greenhouse farming, water recycling systems, and desert agriculture techniques.

Water Independence

World leadership in desalination, wastewater recycling, and smart water management.

The Rise of the “Startup Nation”

Global excellence in Cybersecurity, Medical technology, AI, Semiconductor design, and Ag-Tech.

Economic Prosperity

From modest beginnings in 1948, Israel developed into a high-income, innovation-driven economy and a global technology powerhouse.

Bottom Line

The Eternal Return symbolizes resilience, determination, and the ability to rebuild and flourish after catastrophe.
